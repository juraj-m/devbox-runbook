What runsMemoryCPU

Memory is what stopping it gives back (without the page cache, which the kernel drops anyway). A process's figure is its resident memory, a detail inside its group: shared pages count in each. With processes under it, two figures: its own / with what runs under it. CPU is in cores: 1.0 is one core busy. outside ws: started by hand, not with ws start; grouped by the folder it runs in.

The buttons run ws start, stop, pause, resume or switch on the box as a job of the stack: ws jobs NAME and ws logs NAME start show it there too. Start runs the stack's start commands again even when it runs (they restart what's up). Pause freezes its servers and containers and moves their memory to swap; Resume brings it back in seconds instead of a cold start's minutes. Switch to pauses the other running stacks first. Nothing refuses for lack of memory: the confirmation says what's free and, from the recorded peaks, whether the stack has the room.

Memory at peaks: the control server keeps every sample a minute at a time (ws peaks on the box says the same). A peak is the 99th percentile of the minutes' highest, so one odd minute doesn't set it; More is how much more than now that is. Together they're what to keep free for all of them to peak at once. A stack is active in a minute its servers and containers used twice their median CPU and at least a tenth of a core: some idle servers poll. A Claude session with less than half an hour recorded borrows every session's peak. Paused minutes don't count.

The Claude plan's limits are the account's, as Claude Code's status line last reported them (ws statusline, on a session's reply, here or on the Mac): claude.ai's use counts in them too, and isn't listed. The Mac's sessions arrive within a minute or so of their reply. A session's spend is Claude Code's estimate at list price, a measure of relative use on a subscription, not a bill; its share is of what the listed sessions spent in the window.
